kalshibot

How it works

Futures imply a probability; the fee decides whether to bet#

Each KXFED contract asks whether the upper bound of the Fed's target range will be above a strike after a given meeting. Each 30-day Fed Funds future implies an average rate for its month; in a meeting month the rate after the meeting can be solved from that average, and each meeting's expected move is split between the two nearest 25 bp outcomes and chained forward. A contract's model price is the probability of every outcome above its strike.

Sizing shrinks the model halfway to the market, prices in the fee, and bets a fraction of Kelly:[E3]

p'   = ask + 0.5 × (model − ask)
q    = ask + 0.07 × ask × (1 − ask)          # fee per contract
bet  = bankroll × 0.2 × (p' − q) / (1 − q)   # 0 if p' ≤ q
cap  = $100 per market, $300 across markets
fee  = ceil(0.07 × C × P × (1 − P)) cents      # taker fill of C contracts at P

In the replay, a 25-contract buy at 47¢ pays a 44¢ fee — 3.7% of the stake, which is why the fee enters the price before Kelly sees it.[E2]

What would count as evidence. Settled KXFED contracts, each scored on the bot's last price at least 7 days before settlement, with Brier score and log loss for the model against the market's mid. scripts/evaluate.py computes exactly that; the log has no settlements yet.[E4]

Limits. Futures prices are typed by hand into data/fed_funds_futures.csv; nothing in the code checks how old they are, so a stale file prices every contract from old data.[E5] Futures-implied probabilities include a term premium. Paper mode is the default; live trading needs TRADING_MODE=LIVE and API keys. Not financial advice.
